Interests

I think every researcher has their topics that really spark their intrest. While I have'nt been in the science world long enough to know about every possible research area these 4 subjects I actively try to keep up to date with. Ofcourse this doesn't span my whole interest era!

Evolutionare genetics

I once attended a presentation by a professor who worked in evolutionary genetics. They said something that really stuck with me: “I think we can find the cure to everything if we look at our ancestors, because they also survived.” This stayed with me and sparked my interest in evolutionary genetics. Since then, I've tried to keep up with research on our evolutionary ancestors and the microorganisms that may give us clues about the origins of life. Linked is an article related to how the Last Universal Common Ancestor (LUCA) might have looked like using genomic data for reconstruction.

Coral microbiom

I think bacteria are one of the most interesting organismes on this world. The way they adapt intrests and scares me at the same time. For example how they survive the effects of climate change and how they help corals adapt. But there my intrest in the marine doesnt stop. I also like The PHYVIR project which studie how viruses interact with marine phytoplankton and how these interactions change under environmental stress.

Predicitive artifical intelligence used for drug discovery

In my second year, I studied biological data analysis, which was one of my favorite subjects at school. Not only did it give me a strong foundation in data analysis, but it also sparked my passion for coding. In the summer of 2025, I taught myself Python using online resources and AI as a teacher. This made me see AI as a powerful tool for biological research. I had already used AlphaFold at school, but since then I have been keeping a close eye on the use of predictive AI in biology. One example is this article on structure-aware AI, which predicts how drug molecules interact with proteins. I think this is a very interesting and rapidly growing way of doing biological research.

The use of proteomics to better understand the underlying mechanisms of cancer.

I like research that is focused on better understanding mechanisms of for example cancer. In my freetime I like to read articles on which new pathways are found and how we can use that information in the development of treatments.
